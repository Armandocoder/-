# Offers lens

Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- Clear pain with a real gap: 86% of persona 1 use no tool at all, and the main brake to saving is unexpected family or health costs (71%). Spreadsheets and notebooks are the real substitutes, not funded products.
- Freelancers and traders are the sharper segment: 83% mix business and personal money, 100% want cash-flow forecasting, 83% say they would pay. This is a concrete problem, not a vague wish.
- The price is low and sits inside stated willingness to pay (93% of persona 1 call 2 000 FCFA/month reasonable; budget 2 500-5 000 FCFA for 67% of freelancers). The offer is not competing on price alone: the angle is "no bank link, you stay in control", plus CDP compliance, which respondents asked for.
- Cost to deliver looks low: the plan states a 75.2% contribution margin and the tool stack is mostly free plans. A guarantee would be cheap to offer.
- The tier ladder exists (Free / Premium / Ultra), so there is a way to land small and raise revenue per user.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Likelihood and effort fail the value equation.** 119 of 123 real accounts never recorded one transaction. The product asks the buyer to declare every movement by hand, and the survey said "simple" only after it was explained. Until people complete a first entry and come back in week two, the promised outcome is not believable and the effort is too high.
2. **No proof anyone pays.** Zero paying subscribers, no payment test ever run, and the survey is stated intent from a small convenience sample (n=14, 6 and 3). Persona 3 is three cases and the study itself says not to show investors yet. The plan's break-even of about 970 payers rests on conversion, churn and acquisition cost that appear nowhere in the sources.
3. **The headline outcome is vague and the best feature is not built.** "Ton argent, enfin lisible" is clarity, not a result (saved money, a forecast that holds). The most requested feature for personas 2 and 3, cash-flow forecasting, is Phase 2/3. Ultra at 14 999 FCFA sells a Goals page and one monthly expert appointment, which freelancers rate only moderate, and the partner-offers catalogue is fictional. The top tier has no proven value. There is also no guarantee, no bonus and no real urgency in the offer today.

Also in the way, but behind the three above: no company registration (no NINEA or RCCM), so card and Mobile Money checkout cannot go live; the interim route is manual Wave payment with activation not yet designed. Known defects on first use (missing first-transaction button, fake example numbers flashing on login).

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who is the first paying customer, by name or profile, and where do you reach them? (Channel and acquisition cost are open: run /founder-competitors and /founder-consumer.)
- Why did 119 of 123 sign-ups never enter anything, and what exactly happens in the first five minutes? Is the missing first-transaction button the whole reason, or only part of it?
- What result can a user show after 7 days, and after 30? What is the one outcome you promise, in numbers the user can see?
- Would you refuse payment until a user has recorded entries for two weeks? What guarantee can you afford? (Margin and refund cost: run /founder-cfo.)
- What does a freelancer get at 1 999 FCFA that a spreadsheet and Wave history do not? What would make the tiers worth double?
- Will the Wave and Orange Money apps add budgeting? What stays yours if they do? (Run /founder-competitors.)
- What is the expected free-to-paid conversion and monthly churn, and what is the evidence? (Open; run /founder-consumer for a panel and /founder-cfo for the sensitivity.)
- Is Ultra sold to anyone yet? Who wants the expert appointment, and who delivers it at what cost? (Open.)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Run a real payment test before money is released: a pre-sale or paid pilot (WhatsApp plus Wave is acceptable) with a stated price. The brief does not set a threshold; the founder and the CFO should set the number of paying users, and the board should agree it in advance.
2. Fix activation first: ship the first-transaction fix and show a measured share of new accounts recording an entry and still active in week two. The target share is an open number to set with /founder-cfo and /founder-consumer.
3. Narrow the offer to one buyer and one promised result (freelancers and traders, and a forecast or a clear cash view), and add a guarantee the CFO has costed.
4. Build or drop the top tier: either deliver forecasting and a real expert appointment, or remove Ultra and Premium bonuses that rely on fictional partner offers.
5. Register the company so real checkout can run, and fill the missing numbers (acquisition cost, conversion, churn) with evidence before they are used in the plan.
6. Release funds in stages tied to conditions 1 and 2, not as one 5 000 000 FCFA payment.
