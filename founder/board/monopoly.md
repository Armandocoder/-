# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

The score is for the business as briefed, not for the founder. Every fact below comes from the brief. Where a number is missing, it is named as an open question.

## What is strong
- **A narrow starting wedge.** Voice or text declaration of each movement, in a Mobile Money economy, for people who use no tool today (86% of persona 1; Excel/Sheets and notebooks are the stated substitute). This is a small market that could be owned first: young urban employees and freelancers in Dakar.
- **Timing has a real argument.** Financial inclusion above 80% through Mobile Money, 22.7M mobile connections, and Wave used by 100% of persona 1. The money is already digital but unreadable to its owner.
- **A deliberate design choice that is also a trust angle.** No automatic bank or Mobile Money connection, user controls the data, CDP compliance. Respondents asked for both control and compliance proof. If proof is delivered, it is a small brand asset.
- **Not a payment app.** No BCEAO licence burden, no funds held or moved.
- **Thin cost base.** 75.2% contribution margin in the plan, most tools on free plans.
- **Stated white space.** The study claims no direct competitor in conversational AI personal finance for Senegal. This is unverified.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No durable advantage, and a copycat in month six is easy.** The product is a general AI model (Gemini free tier) behind a declare-and-categorise interface. No proprietary technology 10x better than a notebook or a spreadsheet is evidenced. No network effect: one user's data does not improve another's experience. No scale economy. The brand is not yet earned. Wave, Orange Money and Mixx by Yas already hold the transaction history and the user; the brief says they could add budgeting features. If they do, the wedge becomes a feature of someone else's app. The secret (what the founder knows that others do not) is not stated in the brief.
2. **Distribution is unproven, and the product is not yet a habit.** 119 of 123 real accounts never recorded a single transaction (measured 15/10/2026). Zero paying subscribers, no payment test ever run, no named first paying customer or channel. Customer acquisition cost, churn and free-to-paid conversion are not evidenced anywhere. The plan needs about 970 payers to break even in year 2 and targets 3 000 payers and 50 000 monthly active users by year 3, with no path shown from 4 active accounts to those numbers. Declaring every movement by hand is also the hardest habit to keep, and nobody has measured whether it survives week one.
3. **The plan asks for a monopoly-sized future on evidence that is intent only, and the base is not ready.** Survey n=14, 6 and 3 are small convenience samples of stated intent (the study itself calls persona 3 "3 illustrative cases"). The features the freelancers and wealth personas want most (cash-flow forecasting) are not built. The company is not registered, so card and Mobile Money checkout cannot go live. The repo security audit was "refused", free-tier AI may expose user content to Google, and the admin panel is mostly fake or broken. A product that holds a person's whole financial life cannot afford a trust failure, and the brand rests on trust.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which one of these will you build, and what is the first evidence: proprietary data or know-how, a network effect, scale economies, or a brand? What is the secret others do not know?
- What stops Wave or Orange Money from shipping the same budgeting feature inside the app your users already open daily?
- Who is the exact first market you can own, by name and size (not "Senegal")? How many people are in it?
- How will the first 100 paying customers arrive, through which channel, at what cost each? (Open: acquisition cost, churn, conversion. /founder-consumer and /founder-cfo.)
- Why did 119 of 123 accounts never record one entry, and what happens to that number after the missing first-transaction button is fixed?
- Does declaring entries remain a habit after week one? What is the day-30 return rate?
- Who is actually on Pocket Broker, MyNafa and Djamo, and what do they charge and lack? The "no direct competitor" claim needs checking. (/founder-competitors.)
- What happens to the plan if the free to paid conversion and the price are lower than assumed? (/founder-cfo.)
- Will someone pay before the company is registered, even by manual Wave transfer, and how many by when?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable. The vote is PASS on the current evidence. What would move it to FUND IF, to be proven before any money is released:
- A real payment test: a stated number of strangers (not friends) paying the real price by manual Wave transfer, with the channel and the cost per payer recorded.
- Activation fixed and measured: the share of new accounts that record a first entry, and the share still declaring entries after 30 days.
- One named durable advantage the founder can show, with evidence, and a verified competitor map that includes the mobile money operators' roadmap.
- A defined first market with a size, and a plan to reach the first 100 payers in it.
- Company registered, security audit passed, and the data-protection proof (CDP) shown to users.
