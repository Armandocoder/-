# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The one thing is clear: say what you spent, by voice or text, and see where your money goes. The pitch fits in one sentence ("Ton argent, enfin lisible.").
- The "no" is deliberate: no bank or Mobile Money connection, not a payment app. That is focus, and it keeps Nafa outside payment licensing.
- The experience matches how people already live: Wave is used by 100% of persona 1 and 86% use no tool at all, so the rival is memory, paper and Excel, not a better app.
- 86% of persona 1 find declarative entry simple and fast once explained, and 100% of freelancers are fine with it (self-reported, small samples).
- Trust is treated as part of the product: user control of data, CDP compliance, and respondents asking for proof, not a logo.
- A working production app exists, plus an Android test build, and the founder is fixing defects tab by tab in a set order.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The first five minutes fail.** 119 of 123 real accounts never recorded a single transaction (15/10/2026). The first-transaction button is missing on new accounts, and fake example numbers (about 245 000) flash on login before real data. The core moment is the product, and today most people never reach it. Until this is fixed, every survey figure (93% interested) is unproven.
2. **The habit may not last.** Manual entry depends on the user remembering to do it, every time, with no automatic data to fall back on. Nobody has shown it holds after the first week (the founder's own open question). If it does not, the 1 999 FCFA Premium tier has nothing to sell.
3. **The experience is broken at the seams, and corners are cut where users cannot see.** The security audit of the repo was "refused" (dangerous migration replays). The free-tier Gemini may reuse user content, which contradicts the "you control your data" promise. A wrong price was shown for Ultra (14 704 instead of 14 999). Most admin tabs were fake or broken. Payment is not live (no NINEA/RCCM), the interim WhatsApp + Wave route has no designed way to activate a plan, and plan changes are locked by trigger. The partner-offer entries (Wafa, AXA) are fictional. For a money app, trust is the product, and these are the places a user will lose it.

Also noted: the scope is already wider than the one thing (three tiers, Pro/Perso mode not formalised, expert appointments, a Goals page). The feature freelancers ask for most, cash-flow forecasting, is not built, while the expert appointment only gets moderate interest.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Describe the first five minutes of a new user, second by second. Where does the first entry happen, and what does the user see after it?
- Why did 119 of 123 not enter anything? Have you watched or called any of them? What did they say?
- What brings a user back on day 2, day 7 and day 30, given nothing is connected and nothing happens unless they type or speak?
- What would you cut? If only one of voice entry, budgets, alerts, reports, the AI coach, goals or expert appointments survived, which one?
- Is the 1 999 FCFA Premium plan sold on a feature a free user cannot get, and which one?
- What exactly does Google see of a user's entries today, and what do you tell users about it?
- Who manually activates a paid plan in the interim route, and what does the customer see between payment and access?
- Open questions that need data, not opinion: real acquisition cost, free-to-paid conversion and churn (/founder-consumer to test willingness to pay and retention with fresh buyers, /founder-cfo to price them in, /founder-competitors to check Pocket Broker, MyNafa and the Mobile Money apps' own history views).

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Fix the activation path first (first-transaction button, fake numbers on login), then re-measure the same activation figure on new accounts and share it before more money goes to features or the Android/RevenueCat build.
2. Show that people who make a first entry come back: a retention reading at the end of week 1 on real users, not intentions.
3. Take the AI off the free Gemini tier, or tell users plainly what is sent, and clear the security audit (repo migrations) before any paid launch.
4. Get the company registered and PayTech approved, or design the manual Wave activation end to end, so the first payer does not hit a dead end. Run a real paid test; no one has paid yet.
5. Cut the product to the one thing for launch (declare and see where money goes); put cash-flow forecast, Pro/Perso, goals and expert appointments behind evidence from paying users.
6. Remove or label every fake element a user can see (placeholder partner offers, example numbers, fake admin data) and fix the Ultra price display to 14 999.
7. Release money in stages tied to these checks, not as one 5 000 000 FCFA payment.
